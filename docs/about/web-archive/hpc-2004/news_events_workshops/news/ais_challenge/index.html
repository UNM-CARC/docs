Eldorado High School Team Uses HPC Machines to win New Mexico Adventures in Supercomputing Challenge

 

Professor Susan R. Atlas, an Associated Faculty member of HPC@UNM, and a Research Associate Professor in the Department of Physics and Astronomy, mentored the team that took first place in the 14th Annual Adventures in Supercomputing (AiS) Challenge. This competition, which is sponsored by Los Alamos National Laboratory, is open to high school students throughout the State of New Mexico. The winning team consisted of Daniel Appel, Jeffrey Dimiduk, Thomas Dimiduk and Ryan Shea, all from Eldorado High School in Albuquerque. In addition to winning the overall competition, the team also won the prestigious Cray High Performance Computing award. The winning project was entitled "Atomistic Modeling of Biomolecular Interactions" and was implemented on the LosLobos supercluster and Turing cluster machines at HPC@UNM. The students wrote a modular Fortran90 code to implement the Particle-Mesh Ewald method for computing electrostatic interactions between charged atoms in a system modeled with periodic boundary conditions. The code that they wrote will become part of the larger parallel molecular dynamics simulation code (pdQ) that Atlas is developing at HPC@UNM, along with Dr. Steve Valone of the Materials Science and Technology Division of LANL (currently a Visiting Research Professor in Physics and Astronomy). This larger effort is supported by a research grant from the National Science Foundation under its Nanoscale Science and Engineering Initiative. Atlas and Valone's work is aimed at understanding the physics of molecular motor proteins at the atomic scale, in order to enable the computational design of novel biomimetic materials. Atlas hopes to encourage one or more of the students from the Eldorado team to continue working with her on the project this summer, and longer term, find their way to UNM for their undergraduate or graduate studies. Prof. Atlas is also Director of the UNM Cancer Research and Treatment Center Shared Facility for Bioinformatics and Computational Biology at HPC, which supports computational research in genomics, proteomics, and systems biology.
